Worldcoin & World ID

A dossier investigating Worldcoin and its World ID biometric identity network, examining the use of the "Orb" iris-scanning hardware, regulatory bans, and its integration into global digital verification frameworks.

Introduction

Launched in July 2023 by Tools for Humanity—a company co-founded by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman—Worldcoin is an open-source protocol designed to establish a global financial and identity network. The project expanded significantly throughout 2024, 2025, and into 2026, scanning millions of users' eyes in major urban centers globally. Worldcoin's core system revolves around the "World ID," a digital passport that proves its holder is a real, unique human ("proof of personhood") without revealing their actual identity. Users obtain this ID by presenting their eyes to a physical, spherical iris-scanning device called the "Orb," receiving a allocation of Worldcoin tokens (WLD) in exchange.

Biometric Iris Scanning & 'The Orb'

The "Orb" is a custom-designed biometric imaging device. It scans a user's iris to generate a unique numerical code called an "iris hash." This hash is compared against a database of existing hashes to verify uniqueness. If unique, the user is issued a World ID. While Worldcoin asserts that the raw iris images are deleted once the hash is generated (unless the user opts into "data custody"), security researchers highlight that the deployment of proprietary biometric scanners in public spaces creates unprecedented risk. The centralized coordination of millions of unique biometric identifiers represents a prime target for state intelligence agencies and commercial hackers.

Regulatory Friction and International Bans

Worldcoin's global expansion has encountered major regulatory resistance. Between 2024 and 2026, multiple data protection authorities—including those in Spain, Portugal, Kenya, Hong Kong, and Germany—ordered Worldcoin to halt operations or investigated its consent procedures, particularly regarding the scanning of minors. In Hong Kong, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data (PCPD) declared Worldcoin's collection of iris data unnecessary and excessive, raiding offices and ordering the project to delete scanned records. These interventions highlight a widening conflict between corporate-led biometric networks and territorial data protection laws.

Proof of Personhood in the Age of AI

Sam Altman has explicitly linked the necessity of Worldcoin to the advancement of generative artificial intelligence. As AI makes it increasingly difficult to distinguish human activity from digital bots, Worldcoin is presented as the default infrastructure for verifying human users on the internet. Furthermore, the project is positioned as a potential distribution network for Universal Basic Income (UBI) in an AI-dominated economy. By linking survival resources (UBI) with biometric iris verification, Worldcoin outlines a future where economic participation is conditional upon submitting physical biometric data to private corporations.
