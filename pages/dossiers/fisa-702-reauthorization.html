FISA Section 702 Reauthorization

A dossier investigating the April 2024 reauthorization and expansion of Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, examining the legislative modifications and intelligence surveillance frameworks.

Introduction

On April 20, 2024, the United States Senate passed the Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act (RISAA), formally reauthorizing Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA) for a period of two years. Section 702 permits US intelligence agencies—such as the National Security Agency (NSA)—to conduct warrantless surveillance targeting non-US persons located outside the United States. However, the surveillance inevitably sweeps up the communications of US citizens who interact with those foreign targets, a phenomenon known as "incidental collection." The 2024 legislative renewal represents a significant expansion of the types of businesses forced to cooperate with government surveillance directives.

The Expansion of 'Electronic Communication Service Providers'

The most controversial aspect of the 2024 reauthorization was the modification of the definition of an "electronic communication service provider" (ECSP) under Section 702. Historically, this definition was limited to traditional telecom companies, internet service providers (ISPs), and cloud hosting firms. The new legislative language expands this definition to cover any business or individual with access to equipment that transmits or stores communications. This could include commercial landlords, co-working spaces, hotels, IT service contractors, and small businesses. If served with a directive, these entities must assist the NSA in harvesting communication traffic, turning them into conscripted nodes of the state surveillance apparatus.

Incidental Collection and the Backdoor Search Loophole

US intelligence agencies regularly query Section 702 databases for communications involving US citizens without obtaining a warrant, utilizing a process civil liberties advocates term the "backdoor search loophole." The FBI alone has conducted hundreds of thousands of warrantless searches on US citizens, including activists, protesters, and political donors. Despite attempts by a bipartisan coalition in Congress to attach a warrant requirement to these domestic queries, the final 2024 bill rejected these amendments, codifying warrantless domestic search practices as standard operational procedure for intelligence agencies.

Surveillance and Modern Digital Infrastructure

FISA Section 702 serves as a core legal pillar of the modern global surveillance state. It provides the legal authority for "upstream" collection (interception of data transiting internet fiber-optic cables) and "downstream" collection (direct harvesting from major US technology companies via PRISM). The 2024 reauthorization demonstrates that despite documented abuses and public opposition, the political and intelligence establishment considers warrantless, real-time access to global digital communications an non-negotiable component of state power.
