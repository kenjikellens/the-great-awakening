Sazan Island Luxury Resort Scandal (2024-2026)

A research dossier detailing the environmental and political controversy surrounding Jared Kushner's €1.4 billion luxury resort development on Sazan Island and Vjosa-Narta, the 2024 legislative modifications to Albania's protected areas, and the subsequent 2026 SPAK anti-corruption investigation.

The Sazan Island Status Loophole

Sazan Island, the largest island in Albania, occupies a highly strategic position at the entrance of the Bay of Vlorë. Throughout the 20th century, the island served as a heavily fortified, closed military base under the communist regime of Enver Hoxha. While the marine territory surrounding the island was designated as the Karaburun-Sazan National Marine Park (Category II) in 2010, the terrestrial portion of Sazan itself remained classified as a restricted military zone asset.

This military designation created a critical loophole. Because the land was not formally registered as a terrestrial national park, it sat outside standard environmental protected area constraints. In December 2024, the Albanian government officially declassified Sazan Island from its list of active military assets, immediately rendering the land available for civilian and commercial privatization. Coincidentally, this declassification occurred just as the government granted "strategic investor status" to Atlantic Incubation Partners LLC, a firm representing Jared Kushner’s Affinity Partners, allowing them to bypass standard public bidding and fast-track a €1.4 billion resort development.

2024 Amendments to Protected Area Laws

To facilitate Kushner's development and a sister project in the ecologically sensitive Vjosa-Narta wetland area (near Zvërnec), the Albanian Parliament passed controversial legislative amendments in February 2024 (Law No. 21/2024, amending Law No. 81/2017 "On Protected Areas"). These changes systematically weakened the country's environmental safeguards:

  • Centralized Authority: Final construction and investment approval powers within protected zones were transferred directly to the National Territorial Council (NTC), a body chaired by Prime Minister Edi Rama, effectively stripping the National Agency of Protected Areas (NAPA) of its veto power.
  • Permitting Mega-Resorts: The amendments legalized large-scale "strategic" tourism and infrastructure projects even within strictly protected IUCN Category I and Category II ecological zones.
  • Boundary Redefinition: The NTC was given the authority to redraw protected area boundaries on an ad-hoc basis to accommodate approved development projects.

The 2026 Protests and SPAK Investigation

By late April and early June 2026, the Sazan and Vjosa-Narta projects entered active implementation. When private security and heavy machinery began clearing land and fencing off the Vjosa-Narta wetland, thousands of Albanians took to the streets of Tirana and Vlorë in protest under the slogan "Albania is not for sale." Protesters, led by environmental coalitions like AOS (Albanian Ornithological Society) and EcoAlbania, clashed with police and used symbols such as inflatable flamingos to highlight the threat to migratory species.

In response to the public outcry, the Special Prosecution Office Against Corruption and Organized Crime (SPAK) launched a formal anti-corruption investigation in June 2026. The probe is actively examining the legality of the 2024 legislative changes, potential conflicts of interest, and the administrative processes used to fast-track land titles and licenses for the Kushner-led development.
