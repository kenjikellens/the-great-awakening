Project Mariana

A dossier investigating Project Mariana, a joint initiative by the BIS and major central banks to test the use of decentralized finance (DeFi) protocols, specifically Automated Market Makers (AMMs), for cross-border wholesale CBDC transactions.

Introduction

Project Mariana is a collaborative experiment concluded in late 2023, with final technical reports and integration analyses published throughout 2024. Developed by the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) Innovation Hub Swiss, Eurosystem, and Singapore Centres, in partnership with the Swiss National Bank, Banque de France, and the Monetary Authority of Singapore, the project successfully tested the cross-border settlement of wholesale central bank digital currencies (wCBDCs) using Automated Market Makers (AMMs). This marked the first formal attempt by major central banking authorities to co-opt technology from decentralized finance (DeFi) to optimize foreign exchange settlement.

DeFi Protocols in Central Banking

Automated Market Makers (AMMs) are smart contracts that facilitate token swaps autonomously using mathematical formulas (such as constant product formulas) rather than relying on a traditional order book or centralized intermediaries. In Project Mariana, the participating central banks tested tokenized Swiss francs, euros, and Singapore dollars on a public-private hybrid ledger. The AMM managed the liquidity pools and executed foreign exchange trades automatically. By automating the market-making process, the BIS demonstrated that traditional commercial forex dealers and clearing houses could be replaced by code governed directly by central bank parameters.

Wholesale CBDCs and Centralization

While Worldcoin and retail CBDC proposals focus on public-facing transactions, Project Mariana targets the systemic plumbing of the international banking system. By utilizing wholesale wCBDCs on shared ledger architectures, central banks aim to establish absolute authority over the liquidity corridors linking sovereign currencies. Under this framework, central bank administrators can set real-time compliance rules, restrict currency conversions, or freeze assets directly within the smart contracts. Mariana shows that the "decentralized" tech of DeFi is being co-opted to create more centralized tools of international monetary management.

Strategic Significance

The success of Project Mariana provides the technical foundation for integrating multiple national CBDC networks into unified regional hubs. It shows that central banks are preparing to migrate foreign exchange and sovereign debt trading away from legacy interbank clearing systems toward programmable ledgers. This migration consolidates international monetary flows under the supervision of a tightly knit network of apex central banks, advancing the institutional blueprint for a highly monitored, automated global financial system.
