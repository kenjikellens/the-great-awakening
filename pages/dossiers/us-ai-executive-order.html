White House AI Executive Order 14110

A dossier investigating US Executive Order 14110 on artificial intelligence, analyzing its use of the Defense Production Act, federal reporting thresholds, and content authentication mandates.

Introduction

On October 30, 2023, US President Joe Biden signed Executive Order 14110 on the "Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence." With phased enforcement mandates rolling out across 2024 and 2025, the order establishes a comprehensive federal framework for regulating AI development. The directive relies on the emergency powers of the Korean War-era Defense Production Act to force developers of large AI models to report their training parameters and safety test results to the government. While officially presented as a measure to mitigate existential AI risks, the order establishes government monitoring over private software development.

The Defense Production Act and Reporting Thresholds

The executive order invokes the Defense Production Act (DPA) to mandate reporting for models that meet specific computational thresholds. Developers must notify the federal government when training any model that uses more than 10^26 integer or floating-point operations. In practice, this targets frontier large language models and advanced biological design models. Companies must share their Red-Teaming (safety testing) data before deploying the model publicly. This use of national defense emergency powers to regulate civilian software research establishes a legal precedent for state intervention in private digital research.

Digital Watermarking & Synthetic Content Control

Section 4 of the executive order directs the Department of Commerce to establish standards for detecting AI-generated content and authenticating official government communications. The directive promotes the adoption of digital watermarking and cryptographic content provenance (such as C2PA standards). The stated goal is to protect citizens from AI-enabled fraud and deepfakes. However, civil liberties analysts note that enforcing cryptographic watermarks on all digital media creates the technical foundation for a "verified internet," where unwatermarked, independent media can be automatically flagged, downranked, or censored by web browsers and social platforms.

Centralization of Tech Standards

Executive Order 14110 coordinates its policies through the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) and the Department of Homeland Security (DHS). NIST has established the AI Safety Institute to draft standardized testing rules, while DHS is tasked with incorporating AI safety into national critical infrastructure guidelines. The consolidation of these standards under federal intelligence and security agencies establishes technological progress as a national security issue, integrating tech governance directly with the state security apparatus.
