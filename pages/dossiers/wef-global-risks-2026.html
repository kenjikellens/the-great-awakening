WEF Global Risks (2024-2026)

A dossier tracing the World Economic Forum's annual Global Risks Reports from 2024 to 2026, analyzing the prioritization of AI-driven misinformation and cyber pandemic threats as primary justifications for enhanced global governance.

Introduction

Every January, ahead of its annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland, the World Economic Forum (WEF) publishes its Global Risks Report. Tracing the reports from 2024 through 2026 reveals a significant shift in how the global elite frame the most urgent threats to human civilization. While environmental risks dominate the ten-year outlook, short-term (two-year) horizons are consistently dominated by technological threats—specifically, AI-derived misinformation/disinformation and cyber insecurity. By establishing these threats as systemic crises, the WEF and its network of corporate and state partners build the public case for increased technological monitoring and global digital regulation.

AI Misinformation as a Systemic Threat

The 2024 and 2025 Global Risks Reports elevated "misinformation and disinformation" to the number one short-term risk, outranking extreme weather, societal polarization, and economic downturns. The WEF argues that the proliferation of low-cost generative AI tools allows for the mass creation of deepfakes and manipulated content, threatening the legitimacy of democratic elections and public health mandates. This framing provides institutional legitimacy for public-private initiatives to implement digital content tracing, algorithmic filter regimes, and online identity verification protocols, transforming internet governance into an active censorship system managed by state and tech coalitions.

Anticipation of the 'Cyber Pandemic'

Running alongside misinformation is the threat of "cyber insecurity." WEF risk indices focus heavily on the vulnerability of critical infrastructure—such as electrical grids, water treatment plants, and financial transaction pipelines—to state-sponsored or criminal hacking. In Davos summits, participants frequently warn of an inevitable, systemic "cyber pandemic" that could collapse global supply chains. Critics point out that these warnings mirror the exercises conducted during Cyber Polygon, serving to prime the public to accept centralized, biometric-linked digital IDs and conditional internet access as essential security requirements.

The Agenda of 'Rebuilding Trust'

The overarching theme of the 2024 Davos meeting was "Rebuilding Trust," followed by successive iterations focusing on global coordination. The reports argue that because global risks are interconnected, no single nation-state can manage them in isolation. This analysis leads directly to recommendations for expanded supranational bodies, public-private partnership mandates, and standardized international regulatory codes. By identifying decentralized technology (such as independent cryptography and unregulated AI) as a source of civilizational danger, the WEF actively promotes technocratic oversight as the only viable path forward.
